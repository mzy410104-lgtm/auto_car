**Treat speed measurement and direction detection separately.** The [manufacturer's OPB815 datasheet](https://www.ttelectronics.com/TTElectronics/media/ProductFiles/Datasheet/OPB815.pdf) describes one infrared LED and one NPN phototransistor inside the device. OPB815WZ therefore provides single-channel detection and cannot independently provide A/B phase-based direction detection. Its suitability for measuring speed at this mechanical location does not mean that reverse-direction feedback or complete odometry has been solved for the project. The direction of a control command must not be treated as measured direction. Current limiting, output pull-up/signal conditioning, and wiring to the F103RB still require separate checks; the Grove board's wiring cannot simply be reused.

**Suggested implementation with one photointerrupter: measure speed magnitude with the sensor and use verified motion-control states to provide inferred direction.** With a repeated detection structure having identical, evenly spaced openings, a single pulse channel cannot distinguish forward from reverse rotation. [TI's incremental-encoder explanation](https://www.ti.com/document-viewer/lit/html/SBOA200) distinguishes single-channel speed measurement from direction detection using the phase relationship between two channels. The earlier two-OPB815WZ approach has been withdrawn following the user's space verification and is no longer a purchasing or cover-modification task.

| Control phase | Suggested treatment of speed and direction |
|---|---|
| Steady forward drive | Pulses provide speed magnitude; infer a positive sign from the established forward-control state and identify the control state as the source of direction |
| Request to change from forward to reverse or from reverse to forward | Decelerate and carry out the stopping procedure first; receiving a command for the opposite direction must not immediately change the sign of the recorded speed |
| Braking, neutral, startup, and direction-change transitions | Neutral does not establish that the vehicle is stationary, and a new command's direction is not measured direction; retain raw pulses, mark direction as undetermined during transitions, and do not output a trusted zero speed/displacement when direction is unknown |
| After satisfying stopping criteria calibrated on the actual vehicle | Only then execute the direction-change procedure appropriate to the current TBLE-04S; after entering the opposite drive state, infer the speed sign from that state. Specific control pulse widths, sequences, and delays have not been verified and are not assigned values here |
| Use of signed distance traveled | Accumulate it on this basis only in operating phases where direction estimation is applicable; retain the uncertainty in transition data rather than using it as though the sensor had measured direction |

The no-pulse observation period used in the stopping criteria, minimum resolvable speed, and ESC direction-change behavior require calibration on the actual vehicle. A no-pulse timeout is not strict proof of zero speed, and even steady drive does not rule out external pushing, slipping, or an actuator failing to follow its command. Combining one photointerrupter with control-state information can be an implementation approach for a controlled, low-speed prototype; it does not establish independent detection of actual reverse motion or completed navigation acceptance. If that capability is an acceptance requirement, evidence from another independent motion observation is still needed. Only the sensor model and quantity have been selected at this stage; firmware, wiring, and acceptance requirements have not been changed.
